Retain · EU · stack every weekPrototype ↗15 / 18

A weekly stack takes six steps across two apps today. One approval in the user’s own bank could replace them.

The experiment
BetSet up a weekly standing order from inside Strike: pick €10, €20 or €50 and a day, then approve once in the user’s bank. The bank pushes the money every week and Strike converts it.
TestHalf of new EU users see it at their first deposit. Banks that only allow monthly orders get monthly; the rest get a guided set-up with every detail to copy.
ReadShare of funded users with an active weekly deposit at day 30, then deposits and stack size at day 90. No lift at day 30 after six weeks, it’s off.
AskWhich lane the money lands in: a free recurring buy, about +€2 a user a year at €20 a week, or auto-buy at 0.89%, about +€11. A question for you.
TodaySix steps, two appsCopy the IBAN, add Strike as a payee, schedule a transfer, turn on auto-buy or a recurring buy, match the amounts, keep a buffer.
The ruleBanks have to allow itIf a bank offers standing orders in its own app, it must allow them through a licensed payment-initiation provider (EBA Q&A 2021_6318). Weekly support varies by bank.
Other ways to stack oftenRound-upsOn a card, every payment rounds up into bitcoin (slide 12). From a linked bank, vendor costs of €6–22 a user a year eat what it earns at small amounts.

My proposal, not Strike data. Year one at €20 a week, after a one-off €0.30 set-up, from the model ↗: auto-buy lane +€10.52 to +€11.24, free recurring lane +€1.44 to +€2.17. Direct debit is a poor fit: an eight-week refund right on bitcoin that can’t be returned. Variable weekly pulls (SPAA) are not live yet.
